## 中文版

# 面向 AI 智能体的证据门禁式生命周期治理：框架与医疗器械域实例化

**作者**：赵兴华（Steven Zhao）· MedXpert × SynomosAI · ORCID 0009-0001-0512-1237

### 摘要

AI 智能体（autonomous agents）正在从"被调用"走向"自主行动"，其治理难点不再只是"是否可信"，而是**如何在完整生命周期内持续可证**。现有工作多集中于单点：身份（注册与护照）、证据（审计日志）、或门禁（变更审查）。本文提出 **Evidence-Gated Lifecycle Governance for AI Agents** 框架，主张治理应沿**登记 → 证据 → 门禁 → 退役**四段链条闭合，并以**医疗器械全生命周期监管**作为唯一的现实参照模型完成实例化。

本文与 Yates（AMCIS 2026）提出的 *Evidence-Gated IoT Lifecycle Governance* 存在**明确差异**：后者面向 **IoT 设备**并以 **G1–G7 门禁族**组织；本文面向**自主智能体**并以**注册—证据—门禁（三律）**组织，且进一步给出**离线可核验**与**跨域母版**两个工程要求。本文不主张规则地位，仅提供**实现层（How）**框架。

**关键词**：AI 治理；生命周期治理；证据门禁；自主智能体；医疗器械监管；可追溯性

---

### 1. 引言

#### 1.1 问题

智能体的治理存在一个被忽略的结构性断点：**单点方案之间没有连成链条**。

- 解决**身份**的工作回答"它是谁"；
- 解决**证据**的工作回答"凭什么信"；
- 解决**门禁**的工作回答"谁允许它长"。

但一个智能体从创建到退役，会跨越上述全部三个环节。**注册之后谁管？出事之后证据在哪？能力升级要不要重新审查？退役之后数据与权限如何处置？** 每个问题都有人回答，但极少有工作把它们串成一条生命周期。

#### 1.2 本文贡献

1. 提出面向 AI 智能体的**证据门禁式生命周期治理框架**，以四段链条（登记/证据/门禁/退役）闭合治理闭环；
2. 以**医疗器械全生命周期监管**为现实参照模型（real-world reference model）完成实例化，论证其结构可迁移性；
3. 明确与既有 IoT 版证据门禁工作的**差异边界**，避免概念混同；
4. 给出两条工程要求：**离线可核验**与**证据可携带**。

---

### 2. 相关工作与差异定位

#### 2.1 三条既有脉络

| 脉络 | 代表方向 | 覆盖环节 | 局限 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 身份/注册 | 平台模型登记、数字护照、GB/Z 185—2026 系列（智能体身份码 + 身份管理） | 身份 | 单点；注册后治理缺位 |
| 证据/审计 | 审计日志、来源追溯、可解释性 | 证据 | 证据与动作常不同源，跨系统不可互认 |
| 门禁/对齐 | 变更控制、对齐审查、运行时防护 | 门禁 | 多在企业内部，缺跨域结构 |

#### 2.2 与 Yates（AMCIS 2026）的显式差异

> **这是本文必须讲清的一处边界**：名词相近，**对象与结构均不同**。

| 维度 | Yates：Evidence-Gated IoT Lifecycle Governance | 本文：Evidence-Gated Lifecycle Governance for AI Agents |
|---|---|---|
| **对象** | **IoT 设备**（物理感知/执行节点） | **自主智能体**（可规划、可调用工具、可自演化） |
| **组织方式** | **G1–G7 门禁族** | **三律：注册 / 证据 / 门禁**（四段链条闭合） |
| **生命周期焦点** | 设备部署—运行—维护 | 智能体的**创建—运行—变更—退役**（含**能力扩张**门禁） |
| **离线要求** | 未特别强调 | **显式要求**：无平台依赖、可离线核验 |
| **域实例化** | 未给出受监管行业母版 | **以医疗器械生命周期监管为参照模型** |
| **可迁移性主张** | 面向 IoT 域 | **显式提出跨域母版**（金融/驾驶/机器人/低空等） |

**诚实表述**：本文承认"IoT 版证据门禁"已占该命名组合的 IoT 语义位；本文做的是**对象迁移 + 结构重组 + 域实例化**，**不主张该命名组合的优先权**。

---

### 3. 框架

#### 3.1 三律（Three Laws）

| 法则 | 主张 | 可检验要求 |
|---|---|---|
| **L1 注册（Registry）** | 每个实体在创建时被登记：身份、创造者、治理责任人、声明权限 | 存在唯一标识与责任主体；无豁免 |
| **L2 证据（Evidence）** | 每个关键动作留下可核验证据：决策、风险、变更、人工监督 | 证据与动作同源；可导出、可离线校验 |
| **L3 门禁（Gates）** | 每次演化通过门禁：触发 → 评估 → 放行 → 复核 | 高风险权限扩张须**人工授权**，机器仅可申请 |

#### 3.2 四段链条的闭合

```
创建 ──[L1 注册]──▶ 运行 ──[L2 证据]──▶ 变更 ──[L3 门禁]──▶ 退役 ──[L1'/L2' 注销与证据封存]──▶
```

链条要求**每一段都有可检验产物**，且产物在段间可引用（前一阶段的证据是后一阶段门禁的输入）。

#### 3.3 两条工程要求

- **R1 离线可核验**：核验不依赖特定平台服务；断网可用。
- **R2 证据可携带**：证据格式可导出，且在创建平台之外可独立验证。

> R1/R2 是本文与多数平台内置方案的**主要分野**：平台方案在平台内有效，R1/R2 面向**跨平台、跨组织、断网现场**。

---

### 4. 医疗器械域实例化

医疗器械是**唯一已跑通完整生命周期监管**的行业，因此作为参照模型（structural alignment，**非等价声明**）。

| 智能体生命周期环节 | 医疗器械对应环节 | 可迁移的结构 |
|---|---|---|
| 登记（L1） | 分类界定 + 注册 | 身份与责任主体唯一化；范围声明 |
| 运行证据（L2） | 设计开发记录 / 风险管理（ISO 14971）/ DHR | 动作—证据同源；风险可追溯 |
| 变更门禁（L3） | 变更控制 + 算法变更（如 FDA PCCP 思路） | 变更须评估、须放行、须复核 |
| 人工监督 | 临床评价与上市后监督 | 高风险由人裁定；机器可申请 |
| 退役 | 退市 / 召回 / 报废 | 退役记录与权限吊销路径 |

**据此给出可检验条款（示例）**：
- 任一智能体的权限扩张，**必须**存在一个人工授权记录，且该记录可离线校验；
- 任一关键动作的日志，**必须**可导出为不依赖原平台的格式；
- 退役时，**必须**存在数据处置与权限吊销的闭环记录。

### 5. 边界与未来工作

**边界（诚实声明）**
- 本文是**框架与实例化**，未做大规模实证；有效性需在真实语料与真实部署中检验；
- 与各标准（ISO/IEC 42001、NIST AI RMF、GB/Z 185—2026、EU AI Act）是**对齐关系**，不构成合规保证；
- 参照医疗器械监管是**结构对齐**，不主张法律等价。

**未来工作**
1. 离线核验原型的实证评估（含拒答机制的有效性度量）；
2. 跨域母版在金融 AI 与自动驾驶域的实例化；
3. 证据格式的互认规范与最小字段集。

---

### 6. 结论

AI 智能体的治理问题，正在从"是否可信"转向"**是否持续可证**"。本文主张以**生命周期链条**闭合治理闭环，给出三律框架、医疗器械域实例化与两条工程要求（离线可核验、证据可携带），并明确与既有 IoT 版证据门禁工作的差异。**规则层由标准与监管定义；本文补的是"怎么落"的那一级台阶。**

## English version

# Evidence-Gated Lifecycle Governance for AI Agents: A Framework and a Medical-Device Instantiation

**Author**: Steven Zhao (Zhao Xinghua) · MedXpert × SynomosAI · ORCID 0009-0001-0512-1237

### Abstract

As autonomous agents move from *being called* to *acting on their own*, the governance problem shifts from "can it be trusted?" to "**can it remain provable across its entire lifecycle?**" Existing work addresses single points: identity (registry/passport), evidence (audit logs), or gates (change review). We propose **Evidence-Gated Lifecycle Governance for AI Agents**, arguing that governance must close a four-stage chain — **registry → evidence → gates → retirement** — and instantiate it using **medical-device lifecycle regulation** as the real-world reference model.

We explicitly differentiate this work from Yates (AMCIS 2026), *Evidence-Gated IoT Lifecycle Governance*: Yates targets **IoT devices** organized by an **G1–G7 gate family**; ours targets **autonomous agents** organized by **three laws (registry / evidence / gates)**, and adds two engineering requirements — **offline verifiability** and **portable evidence**. We claim no rule-setting authority; the contribution is an **implementation layer (How)**.

**Keywords**: AI governance; lifecycle governance; evidence-gated; autonomous agents; medical device regulation; traceability

### 1. Introduction

The structural gap: **single-point solutions were never chained.** Identity work answers *who is it*; evidence work answers *prove it*; gate work answers *who lets it grow*. But one agent crosses all three. Who governs it after registration? Where is the evidence after an incident? Must a capability upgrade be re-reviewed? What happens to data and permissions at retirement? Each question has an answer; few connect them into a single lifecycle.

**Contributions**: (1) a lifecycle framework for AI agents closing the registry/evidence/gates/retirement loop; (2) a medical-device instantiation as a real-world reference model; (3) an explicit boundary against the IoT version of evidence-gated governance; (4) two engineering requirements — offline verifiability and portable evidence.

### 2. Related Work and Differentiation

**Three strands**: identity/registry; evidence/audit; gates/alignment — each strong as a point, weak as a chain.

**Explicit difference from Yates (AMCIS 2026)**

| Dimension | Yates (IoT) | This work (AI Agents) |
|---|---|---|
| Target | IoT devices | Autonomous agents (plan, call tools, self-evolve) |
| Organization | G1–G7 gate family | Three laws: registry / evidence / gates |
| Lifecycle focus | deploy–operate–maintain | create–operate–**change**–retire (incl. capability expansion) |
| Offline requirement | not emphasized | **explicit**: no platform dependency, offline verifiable |
| Domain instantiation | none | **medical-device lifecycle regulation** |
| Transferability claim | IoT domain | **explicit cross-domain template** |

We acknowledge the IoT semantic slot is occupied and claim no priority over the naming combination; our contribution is **object transfer + structural recomposition + domain instantiation**.

### 3. Framework

**Three laws** — L1 Registry (identity, creator, governance owner, declared scope; no exemption); L2 Evidence (evidence co-originated with action; exportable and offline-checkable); L3 Gates (trigger → assessment → release → review; high-stakes expansion requires **human authorization**).

**Chain closure**: create →[L1]→ operate →[L2]→ change →[L3]→ retire →[revoke + seal evidence].

**Two engineering requirements**: R1 offline verifiability; R2 portable evidence. These are the primary distinction from platform-embedded approaches.

### 4. Medical-Device Instantiation

Medical devices are the one industry that already runs full-lifecycle regulation; we use it as structural alignment (not an equivalence claim).

| Agent stage | Medical-device analogue | Transferable structure |
|---|---|---|
| Registry (L1) | classification + registration | unique identity + responsible party |
| Evidence (L2) | design records, ISO 14971 risk management, DHR | action–evidence co-origin |
| Gates (L3) | change control, algorithmic change (PCCP-style) | assess, release, review |
| Human oversight | clinical evaluation, post-market surveillance | high stakes decided by humans |
| Retirement | withdrawal / recall / disposal | revocation + data disposition |

**Sample testable clauses**: any permission expansion must have an offline-verifiable human-authorization record; any key action log must be exportable in platform-independent form; retirement must close a data-disposition and revocation loop.

### 5. Boundaries and Future Work

This is a framework and instantiation, not a large-scale empirical study. Relationships to ISO/IEC 42001, NIST AI RMF, GB/Z 185—2026 and the EU AI Act are **alignment, not compliance guarantees**. Future work: empirical evaluation of an offline verification prototype; instantiation in financial AI and autonomous driving; a minimal interoperation field set for evidence.

### 6. Conclusion

Agent governance is shifting from *trustworthiness* to **continuous provability**. We close the loop with a lifecycle chain, three laws, a medical-device instantiation and two engineering requirements — while making our difference from IoT evidence-gated work explicit. **Standards and regulators define the rules; this work supplies the implementation step.**
